How it works

You set up a permanent workspace once. After that, every job takes about a minute.

  1. Create a workspace in your AI app

    A Project in Claude or ChatGPT, or a Gem in Gemini. It remembers the instructions and your resume.

  2. Add the ResumeMachine instructions and your base resume

    The instructions are a plain text file you can read in full. Your resume goes straight into your own AI account.

  3. Paste a job posting

    Open a new chat in the workspace, paste the full posting, and review what comes back.

Tips for better results

  • Make your base resume a master copy. Long is fine. Include every role, project, and win you might want to use. ResumeMachine picks what's relevant; it can't add what isn't there.
  • Include numbers. Team sizes, budgets, percentages, timeframes. These make the strongest bullets.
  • Paste the full posting text, not just a link. Many job sites block AI assistants from reading them.
  • Answer its questions. When it asks for a detail, your answer makes the resume better. Add it to your base resume too.
  • Always read the final version. It's your name on it. Check every claim before you send it.

Questions

Where does my resume go?

Only into your own AI account (Claude, ChatGPT, or Gemini), the same as any file you share with it. This site is a static page with no backend, no forms, and no analytics. It never sees your resume or the jobs you apply to.

Does it work on free plans?

Usually. Saved workspaces (Projects, Gems, custom GPTs) are available on many plans, though free plans may limit them or leave them out. If yours doesn't have one, paste the instructions at the start of each chat instead; it works the same, just with one extra step.

Will it make things up to fit the job?

It's instructed not to. It can rephrase, reorder, and reframe what's in your base resume, but it won't invent jobs, titles, dates, skills, or numbers. When a bullet needs a number it doesn't have, it leaves a placeholder like [X%] and asks. AI can still make mistakes, so read the final version before you send it.

Will it get me past screening software (ATS)?

It uses the posting's own wording where it's true for you and keeps formatting plain (no tables, columns, or graphics), which is what screening software parses best. No tool can guarantee a result, and a referral from someone inside the company does far more than any keyword.

Why isn't this just a website that does it for me?

Because the AI you already use is better at it than anything a small site could build, and your data stays in an account you already trust. ResumeMachine is the part that's hard to get right on your own: a well-tested set of instructions and a setup that takes three minutes.

Can it write cover letters too?

Yes. Every reply includes a few cover-letter angles, and you can ask for a full letter, interview prep, or a LinkedIn summary in the same chat.

What should go in my base resume?

Everything. Think of it as a master copy: every role, project, metric, and win you might ever want to use. Long is fine. ResumeMachine picks what's relevant for each job, but it can't add what isn't there.
